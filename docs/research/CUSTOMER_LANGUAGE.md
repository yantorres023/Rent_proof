## 3. Vocabulary patterns (INFERENCE from the titles above)

- **US renters say:** "security deposit", "damage deposit", "move-in checklist", "move in inspection", "move out charges", "pre-existing damage", "charging extra", "keeping my deposit", "carpet cleaning", "painting", "wear and tear".
- **UK renters say:** "deposit deductions", "inventory", "check-in / check-out", "DPS" (or TDS / mydeposits), "letting agent", "previous tenants' mess", "inventory not signed", "unfair".
- **Emotional register:** "screw me", "unfair", "accusations", "huge", "trying to keep". Anger and powerlessness are framed around *being blamed for someone else's damage*.
- **Anxiety triggers:** the landlord never gave a checklist; a missing or unsigned inventory; a change of agent. These describe situations where **no reliable landlord record exists**, which is where tenant evidence matters most.
- **Cost categories named:** cleaning, painting, carpet. This is consistent with scheme data showing cleaning in 54% of TDS disputes [45] and cleaning and damage as the top DPS causes [44].

## 4. Implications for copy (HYPOTHESIS)

- Use "security deposit" (US) and "deposit deductions / inventory" (UK). Avoid "evidence chain" and "hash" jargon in top-level copy.
- A candidate hook in customers' own words: "Prove it was already like this when you moved in." This paraphrases the [SNIPPET] above and has not been tested.
- Name the cost categories people actually get charged for (cleaning, carpet, paint, walls) in the capture guidance.
