# EVIDENCE_LEDGER_MARKET

Date: 2026-09-25. Source numbers [n] refer to SOURCES.md. All evidence was seen in search-result summaries only; WebFetch was blocked (see SOURCES.md, method note). Copies of the same claim are not counted as independent evidence: Big Issue [51] repeats Generation Rent [50], and the RapidEye aggregator [43] repeats Zillow [40][42].

### A1. Renters lose deposit money to disputes or deductions
- **ASSUMPTION:** A meaningful share of renters lose some or all of their deposit at move-out, and some of those deductions are contestable.
- **SUPPORTING EVIDENCE:**
  - US: ~40–42% of recent movers got the entire deposit back, so ~58–60% did not (Zillow CHTR 2024 via Zillow guide and aggregator) [42][43].
  - England: 35% had ever lost some or all of a deposit (Nationwide, 2018, n>2,000) [52]; 23% experienced deductions they thought unreasonable (Generation Rent supporter survey) [50]; landlords themselves report returning deposits in full in only ~59% of cases (EPLS via secondary source) [53].
  - Ireland: deposit retention was 19% of RTB disputes in 2024 (1,811 cases) [54].
- **CONTRARY EVIDENCE:**
  - Formal disputes are rare: 1% of deposits go to TDS adjudication [45], and DPS says under 3% are disputed [44].
  - Many deductions may be legitimate; neither the "not full" figures nor the landlord surveys separate fair deductions from unfair ones.
  - Ontario and Québec have no damage deposits [33][34].
- **SOURCE LINKS:** [42][43][44][45][50][52][53][54][33][34]
- **SOURCE TYPE:** COMMERCIAL-SURVEY (Zillow, Nationwide); NGO self-selected survey (Generation Rent); GOV-STAT via secondary source (EPLS); SCHEME (TDS, DPS, RTB)
- **SOURCE DATE:** 2018–2025
- **CONFIDENCE:** MEDIUM (that partial loss is common); LOW (that the loss is *unfair* or contestable)
- **STATUS:** SUPPORTED (loss happens) / WEAK (unfair share unknown)
- **WHAT WOULD FALSIFY IT:** Representative data showing that more than ~85% of deposits are returned in full, or that nearly all deductions are uncontested and legitimate.

### A2. Documentation helps in disputes
- **ASSUMPTION:** Tenant-held, dated photo documentation of move-in condition improves outcomes in deposit disputes.
- **SUPPORTING EVIDENCE:**
  - Adjudicators "rely 100 per cent on the documents"; date-stamped photos are listed evidence [47][48].
  - Several statutes turn on documented condition: CA AB 2801 requires landlord photos to support deductions [13][14]; WA voids deposit retention without a checklist [20]; BC extinguishes the damage claim without an inspection report [30].
  - Zillow: renters who used a checklist, photos, video and told the landlord had ~50% odds of a full refund, vs ~40% overall (correlational) [42].
  - Courts publish digital-evidence guidance for small claims [58][59].
- **CONTRARY EVIDENCE:**
  - The Zillow effect is correlational: diligent renters may also be cleaner tenants.
  - In England the burden of proof is already on the landlord (per MSE forum snippets [103–108]; the same principle applies in Germany [37]), so tenant photos add less.
  - In GA and KY, signed landlord lists are "conclusive", which can override tenant photos if the tenant fails to dissent [22][23].
- **SOURCE LINKS:** [13][14][20][22][23][30][42][47][48][58][59]
- **SOURCE TYPE:** STATUTE, SCHEME, GOV-GUIDE (court), COMMERCIAL-SURVEY
- **SOURCE DATE:** 2024–2026
- **CONFIDENCE:** MEDIUM-HIGH (documentation matters structurally); LOW (size of the effect)
- **STATUS:** SUPPORTED
- **WHAT WOULD FALSIFY IT:** Adjudication or court outcome data showing no difference in awards between tenants with and without move-in photos.

### A3. Renters currently fail to document adequately
- **ASSUMPTION:** Many renters do not create organized move-in documentation.
- **SUPPORTING EVIDENCE:**
  - Forum titles about having no checklist or inspection [98][99][102].
  - Universities repeatedly publish "take photos at move-in" advice, which implies the gap is common [112–115].
  - 46% don't know they can challenge deductions [49], which suggests low process literacy.
  - One search summary said skipping documentation is "the most common reason a security deposit gets partially withheld". The source is unclear (probably a vendor blog), so it is **not relied on**.
- **CONTRARY EVIDENCE:**
  - Smartphone ownership is ~91% [3], and many renters probably do take camera-roll photos. One timestamp-camera app alone has ~90M downloads [83].
  - In England and BC, the landlord or joint inventory often exists [30][47].
- **SOURCE LINKS:** [3][30][47][49][83][98][99][102][112–115]
- **SOURCE TYPE:** FORUM, University guidance, NGO survey, AGGREGATOR
- **SOURCE DATE:** various, up to 2025
- **CONFIDENCE:** LOW
- **STATUS:** WEAK / UNVALIDATED_WITH_REAL_USERS
- **WHAT WOULD FALSIFY IT:** A survey or interviews showing that most renters already keep complete, retrievable, room-by-room move-in photos.

### A4. Existing apps don't serve tenants well
- **ASSUMPTION:** There is no good tenant-first inspection app, which leaves a gap.
- **SUPPORTING EVIDENCE:**
  - Major tools are landlord-paid, and the data lives in the landlord's account: RentCheck [63][64], zInspector [68], HappyCo [69], TurboTenant/Avail [79][80], UK inventory software [70–72].
  - RentCheck reviews mention lost photos and upload failures [66][67].
  - No tenant-first app showed large scale in the data seen; Tenant Inspect had "300 downloads" per a third-party estimate [88].
- **CONTRARY EVIDENCE:**
  - **At least 8 tenant-first apps exist**, several with rich features: Rentproof (same name, local-only, $4.99) [86]; MoveSnap (comparison slider, dispute bundle, QR verification) [87]; DepositGenie (AI rebuttal, deadlines) [89]; Tenant Inspect [88]; MoveProof [91]; ProofSnap [93]; others [92].
  - The feature gap is small. The unsolved problem is traction and distribution.
- **SOURCE LINKS:** [63–69][79][80][86–93]
- **SOURCE TYPE:** VENDOR, AGGREGATOR
- **SOURCE DATE:** 2020–2026
- **CONFIDENCE:** MEDIUM
- **STATUS:** CONTRADICTED (as a feature gap) / WEAK (as a distribution gap)
- **WHAT WOULD FALSIFY IT (the remaining distribution-gap version):** Evidence that an existing tenant app has large active usage (e.g. >50k ratings), or strong partnerships with universities or tenant unions.

### A5. Renters will use a guided app
- **ASSUMPTION:** Renters will complete a guided room-by-room capture on move-in day.
- **SUPPORTING EVIDENCE:**
  - RentCheck's resident-led model shows renters complete guided inspections when the landlord requires it: ~89k Android downloads, 4.52★ [65], and a vendor-reported resident satisfaction of 4.8/5 [66].
  - TurboTenant and Avail ask tenants to complete checklists [79][80].
- **CONTRARY EVIDENCE:**
  - The RentCheck usage is *mandated by the landlord*, not voluntary.
  - Voluntary tenant apps show low traction [88].
  - The generic timestamp camera's popularity [83] suggests renters prefer zero-setup tools.
- **SOURCE LINKS:** [65][66][79][80][83][88]
- **SOURCE TYPE:** AGGREGATOR, VENDOR
- **SOURCE DATE:** 2025–2026
- **CONFIDENCE:** LOW
- **STATUS:** UNVALIDATED_WITH_REAL_USERS
- **WHAT WOULD FALSIFY IT:** In a pilot, fewer than ~30% of installers complete one full move-in inspection, or median completion time is far above what users will tolerate.

### A6. Renters will pay
- **ASSUMPTION:** Renters will pay for a documentation app (one-time ~$4.99–$9.99).
- **SUPPORTING EVIDENCE:**
  - Competitors price one-time: Rentproof $4.99 [86], Tenant Inspect $19.99 [88], MoveSnap Pro one-time [87], DepositGenie one-time plans [89].
  - The stake is a typical deposit of $795 [41].
  - Renters already pay monthly fees for deposit alternatives such as Rhino (~$10–20/mo) [84] (a different job).
- **CONTRARY EVIDENCE:**
  - No revenue, conversion or ratings-volume data was found for any tenant app.
  - Tenant Inspect at $19.99 has a low download estimate [88].
  - Free substitutes (camera, timestamp apps) are strong [83].
  - Renters who already use RentCheck through their property manager get guidance free [63].
- **SOURCE LINKS:** [41][63][83][84][86–89]
- **SOURCE TYPE:** VENDOR, AGGREGATOR, COMMERCIAL-SURVEY
- **SOURCE DATE:** 2024–2026
- **CONFIDENCE:** LOW
- **STATUS:** UNVALIDATED_WITH_REAL_USERS
- **WHAT WOULD FALSIFY IT:** A fake-door or paywall test showing under ~1–2% conversion among move-in-stage users, or interviewees who say they would not pay even at move-out.

### A7. Photos, timestamps and hashes are legally useful
- **ASSUMPTION:** Timestamped photos with SHA-256 hashes of the originals strengthen the tenant's position in disputes and courts.
- **SUPPORTING EVIDENCE:**
  - Scheme guidance lists date-stamped photos as evidence [47].
  - Small claims courts accept digital evidence and relax formal rules [58][59].
  - Statutes rely on photos (CA AB 2801) [13].
- **CONTRARY EVIDENCE:**
  - A vendor source says self-declared EXIF rarely authenticates on its own under FRE 901 if contested [60] (vendor with an interest, LOW).
  - A locally computed SHA-256 shows that a file has not changed *since hashing*. It does **not** prove capture time unless it is anchored externally (e.g. qualified or RFC 3161 timestamps [93], C2PA [81][82]).
  - Landlords can also fabricate (the TikTok case [95]); authenticity cuts both ways.
- **SOURCE LINKS:** [13][47][58][59][60][81][82][93][95]
- **SOURCE TYPE:** SCHEME, GOV-GUIDE (court), STATUTE, VENDOR, NEWS
- **SOURCE DATE:** 2024–2026
- **CONFIDENCE:** MEDIUM (photos are useful); LOW (hashes add legal weight without external anchoring)
- **STATUS:** SUPPORTED (photos) / WEAK (hash-only integrity claims)
- **WHAT WOULD FALSIFY IT:** Adjudicator or judge guidance, or tenant-lawyer feedback, that hash manifests are ignored. Also: evidence that tenant photos are routinely rejected as unauthenticated in small claims.
- **NOTE:** Get a licensed attorney's review before using any "court-ready" or "legally binding" copy.

### A8. The market size is adequate
- **ASSUMPTION:** The beachhead has enough renters with deposits, and enough move-ins per year, to support the product.
- **SUPPORTING EVIDENCE:**
  - US: ~44.6M rented units [1]; 83% of recent renters paid a deposit [41]; 44% of California households rent [2].
  - England: 4.7M PRS households and 4.7M protected deposits [4][46].
  - Germany: ~19.9M tenant households [11].
- **CONTRARY EVIDENCE:**
  - The addressable *paying* segment is unknown (see A6).
  - Canada is fragmented by provincial deposit bans [33][34].
  - Formal disputes are only 1–3% in England [44][45].
  - I found no annual move count for renters in these sources; the move rate is an estimate I have not sourced.
- **SOURCE LINKS:** [1][2][4][11][33][34][41][44][45][46]
- **SOURCE TYPE:** GOV-STAT, COMMERCIAL-SURVEY, SCHEME
- **SOURCE DATE:** 2021–2025
- **CONFIDENCE:** HIGH (the top of the funnel is large); LOW (the monetizable segment)
- **STATUS:** SUPPORTED (top of funnel) / UNVALIDATED_WITH_REAL_USERS (monetizable segment)
- **WHAT WOULD FALSIFY IT:** Measured acquisition costs or conversion rates implying revenue per US move-in cohort below what sustains the product.

  - There is also a "RentProof AI" App Store listing [92].
  - "Rent Proof" is also used by rentapplication.net (search title only).
- **SOURCE LINKS:** [86][92]
- **SOURCE TYPE:** VENDOR
- **SOURCE DATE:** 2025–2026
- **CONFIDENCE:** HIGH (that a conflict exists)
- **STATUS:** CONTRADICTED
- **WHAT WOULD FALSIFY IT:** Trademark clearance and store checks showing that the existing listing has been withdrawn, or that there is no confusing similarity (unlikely).
