# MARKET — RentProof market comparison and beachhead

Date: 2026-09-25. Source numbers [n] refer to SOURCES.md. All evidence comes from search-result summaries, because WebFetch was blocked (see SOURCES.md, method note). Each claim is labelled **FACT** (stated by a cited source), **INFERENCE** (my reasoning from facts) or **HYPOTHESIS** (untested). No user interviews were done: every user-behaviour statement is UNVALIDATED_WITH_REAL_USERS.

## 1. Market facts by country

### United States
- **Renter households.** FACT: ACS 2019-2023 shows ~44.6M rented units [1]. FACT: 44% of California households rent vs 35% in the rest of the US [2].
- **Deposit norms.**
  - FACT: the typical deposit was $795 in 2025, and 83% of recent renters paid one (Zillow, commercial survey) [41]; $750 in 2024 [40].
  - FACT: California AB 12 caps deposits at 1 month's rent from 1 Jul 2024, or 2 months for small landlords [18][19].
  - FACT (LOW, aggregator): about 20 states have no statutory cap, e.g. TX, FL, WA [26].
- **Deposit loss.**
  - FACT (commercial survey, MEDIUM-LOW): Zillow reports that roughly 40–42% of recent movers got their entire deposit back [42][43].
  - FACT (MEDIUM-LOW): the same summary says renters who used a checklist, photos, video and told the landlord had ~80% odds of getting some back and ~50% of getting all of it [42]. This is correlational, the method is unknown, and I could not identify the exact page.
- **Statutory move-in documentation (duty falls on the landlord).**
  - California AB 2801 (FACT [13][14][15]):
    - Landlords must photograph the unit before or at the start of tenancy (tenancies from 1 Jul 2025), at move-out, and after repairs (from 1 Apr 2025).
    - Photos must accompany the itemized statement due within 21 days.
    - Bad-faith non-compliance can forfeit the right to deduct.
    - Tenants can request a pre-move-out inspection.
  - Washington RCW 59.18.260 (FACT [20]): no deposit may be collected without a written, signed checklist. If the landlord fails, it is liable for the whole deposit.
  - Massachusetts c.186 §15B (FACT [21]): the landlord must give a statement of condition within 10 days; the tenant may return a list of damage within 15 days.
  - Georgia 44-7-33 (FACT [22]): a list of existing damage must be given before the deposit is paid; the tenant may dissent in writing. If the tenant signs the final list without dissenting, they lose the right to recover.
  - Kentucky 383.580 (FACT [23]): similar to Georgia.
  - Virginia 55.1-1226 (FACT [24]): the landlord reports existing damage within 5 days; the report is "deemed correct" unless the tenant objects within 5 days.
  - FACT (LOW; search summary of a Nolo/other listing): around 17 states require a checklist [25].
- **Dispute route.** FACT: disputes go to demand letters and small claims, which relax formal evidence rules [58][59]. INFERENCE: there is no free, deposit-scheme-style adjudicator, so the burden and effort fall on the tenant. That makes the tenant's own evidence more valuable than in England.
- **Smartphone use.** FACT: 91% of US adults own a smartphone [3].
- **Complaints to regulators.** I could not find a sourced CFPB or state AG count for deposit complaints. The one figure I saw (NY) had no attributable source and is excluded [117].

### England (UK)
- **Renter households.** FACT: the private rented sector is 4.7M households (19%) in 2024-25 [4]. FACT: 4.7M deposits are protected in England & Wales [46].
- **Deposit norms.** FACT: the cap is 5 weeks' rent (6 if annual rent ≥ £50k) [27]. FACT: the Renters' Rights Act 2025 came into force on 1 May 2026 and leaves the deposit framework largely unchanged [28][29].
- **Deposit loss.**
  - FACT (Nationwide via press, 2018, n>2,000; LOW-MEDIUM and dated): 35% had ever lost some or all of a deposit [52].
  - FACT (Generation Rent / Opinium, n=2,000, nationally representative, 2025; MEDIUM; advocacy sponsor): 46% didn't know they could challenge deductions [49].
  - FACT (Generation Rent supporter survey, self-selected; LOW): 23% had deductions they felt unreasonable; 56% got the full deposit back [50][51].
  - FACT (landlord-reported EPLS via a secondary source; MEDIUM; year unverified): 59% of landlords returned the deposit in full [53].
- **Dispute visibility.** This is high: the schemes publish statistics.
  - FACT: 1% of deposits (46,950) went to formal adjudication in the year to Mar 2025 [45].
  - FACT: DPS says under 3% of deposits are disputed [44].
  - FACT: cleaning appears in 54% of TDS cases [45].
- **Evidence regime.** FACT: adjudication is a paper review on the balance of probabilities. Adjudicators "rely 100 per cent on the documents", and date-stamped photos are listed evidence [47][48]. FACT: forum advice says the onus of proof is on the landlord [103–108].
- **Inventory culture.** INFERENCE (from scheme and industry content [47][70][71][72]): landlords and agents usually commission check-in inventories, so tenant value moves to annotating or disputing the landlord's inventory, not creating the only record. I found no official statistic on how many tenancies have an inventory; the EPLS search did not surface one.
- **Smartphone use.** FACT (LOW, aggregator): 93% of mobile users own a smartphone [6].

### Canada
- **Renter households.** FACT: ~5M renter households (~33%) in the 2021 Census [8][9].
- **Deposit norms by province.**
  - FACT: Ontario forbids damage deposits; only a last-month's-rent deposit is allowed [33].
  - FACT: Québec forbids requiring any deposit (CCQ art. 1904) [34][35].
  - FACT: British Columbia caps the deposit at half a month's rent and requires a joint condition inspection report (RTB-27). A landlord who fails to provide it within 7 days loses the right to claim damage from the deposit [30][31].
- INFERENCE: the two largest provinces (Ontario and Québec) have little or no damage deposit to protect. The deposit-protection value therefore mostly applies to BC and the Prairies/Atlantic provinces, and the market is effectively fragmented.
- **Smartphone use.** FACT (via trade association citing Pew): 95–98% among under-50s [10].

### Germany
- **Renter households.** FACT: ~19.9M main-tenant households (2022); Germany is the EU's biggest renter country [11][12].
- **Deposit norms.** FACT: up to 3 months' net cold rent, payable in 3 instalments (§ 551 BGB) [36].
- **Handover protocol.** FACT: the Übergabeprotokoll is not legally required, but it is common practice. A protocol signed by both parties is presumed correct, and the landlord bears the burden of proof for damage [37][38].
- INFERENCE: this is a high-stakes deposit market (3 months' rent) with strong protocol culture. However, it needs German-language legal content and has high localization cost.

### Ireland
- FACT: deposit retention was the 2nd most common RTB dispute type in 2024 (1,811 applications, 19%) [54][55].
- FACT: a Protection of Tenants' Deposits Bill 2025 is in progress [39].
- FACT: at least two Irish-specific evidence apps already exist (ProofSnap, TenureFolio) [93][94].
- INFERENCE: this is a small market (roughly 0.3–0.4M private tenancies, which is my estimate and unsourced here).

### Search-demand clues

- Proxies (INFERENCE, LOW):
  - Many universities, legal publishers and vendors publish "how to get your security deposit back" and move-in checklist content [25][112–115].
  - At least 8 tenant-side apps launched, many recently [86–92].
  - A viral TikTok about a deposit scam got mainstream coverage [95].

Scoring notes:
- Scores are my judgment calls (INFERENCE) from the cited facts. Criteria 10 and 11 rest on especially thin evidence.
- **US criterion 5:** AB 2801 cuts both ways. Landlord photos reduce the tenant's documentation gap, but they are controlled by the landlord and usually seen only after a deduction. In Georgia and Virginia, the 5-day or pre-deposit dissent windows make a fast, tenant-side capture more valuable, because a missed objection counts against the tenant [22][24] (INFERENCE).
- **England:** the evidence system is the most tenant-friendly (free, evidence-based, burden on landlord). That lowers the harm per renter and reduces the need for a separate app, because the landlord's inventory usually exists.

## 3. Beachhead recommendation

**Recommended beachhead: United States. Young and first-time renters, especially students and recent graduates, who are moving into a privately managed apartment or house, captured in the first 72 hours after getting the keys.**

| Field | Content |
|---|---|
| Country | United States (English-only). Launch content should be neutral about state law and make no legal guarantees. It can mention that some states (e.g. VA 5-day, MA 15-day, GA pre-deposit) set short objection windows [21][22][24]. |
| Customer | Renters aged 18–34, first or second lease, with a deposit of ~$500–$1,500 [41]. HYPOTHESIS: they are the least experienced and most likely to pay a deposit. FACT: 83% of Gen Z recent renters paid a deposit vs 79% of Gen X/Boomers [41]. |
| Context | Unfurnished or lightly furnished apartment from a small landlord or mid-size property manager that does not offer RentCheck or zTenant (INFERENCE: tenants whose manager already uses RentCheck get a guided flow free, because the landlord pays [63][64]). |
| Trigger | Key handover / move-in day. The secondary trigger is receiving the landlord's move-in checklist or damage report, which has deadlines in some states [22][24]. |
| Problem | At move-out months later, the renter cannot prove what was pre-existing. Unorganized camera-roll photos are hard to find, lack room context, and are easy to contest. |
| Current alternative | Phone camera roll and video walk-throughs (FACT: widely advised by university legal offices [112][113][115]); timestamp-camera apps (FACT: one has ~90M downloads [83]); paper checklists from the landlord; doing nothing. |
| Why insufficient | INFERENCE / HYPOTHESIS: the photos are not organized by room or item, there is no before/after pairing, and no report can be handed over. Watermark-only timestamps are easy to fake. EXIF alone is weak if authenticity is contested (vendor claim [60]; small claims is more lenient [58][59]). Renters forget, or do only a partial walkthrough. |
| Expected value | HYPOTHESIS: better recovery of a ~$795 typical deposit [41]. Zillow's correlational figure (~50% full refund for renters who documented thoroughly, vs ~40% overall [42]) suggests a meaningful lift, but it is **not causal**. |
| Payment hypothesis | HYPOTHESIS: free capture, then a one-time unlock (~$4.99–$9.99) for the PDF report and move-out comparison, bought at the moment of need (move-out or dispute). Anchors: Rentproof $4.99 [86], Tenant Inspect $19.99 [88]. Evidence of willingness to pay is weak; see the ledger. |
| Distribution hypothesis | HYPOTHESIS: university off-campus housing offices and student legal services, which already publish "document your move-in" checklists [112–115]; tenant unions [61]; SEO on "security deposit" questions (no volume data obtained); App Store search for "move in inspection". |

**Why the US over England.** Evidence-weighted reasons:
1. There are about 9× more renter households [1][4].
2. There is no free adjudication scheme, so the tenant's own organized evidence carries more of the load.
3. Most US states do not require a landlord checklist, so there is often no landlord-provided record [25].
4. Deposit loss appears common [42][43], although that figure comes from a commercial survey.

**Runner-up: England private renters, as an "inventory companion".** The tenant checks and annotates the agent's check-in inventory within the comment window, then assembles a DPS/TDS/mydeposits evidence pack at check-out. It has the best official statistics and a document-driven adjudication process [44–48]. It ranks second because inventories are usually provided, formal disputes are only 1–3% of deposits [44][45], and harm per renter is capped at 5 weeks' rent [27].

**Critical caveats.**
1. An iOS app already named **"Rentproof"** (getrentproof.app) has nearly identical positioning: guided rooms, timestamps and GPS, move-in vs move-out comparison, PDF, on-device only, $4.99 one-time [86]. There is also a "RentProof AI" App Store listing [92]. **Trademark and store-name conflict risk is HIGH, and differentiation must be explicit.**
2. The tenant-first category is crowded with indie apps that show little evidence of traction (see COMPETITORS.md). The main risk is distribution and willingness to pay, not feature gaps.
3. Confidence in the beachhead choice is **MEDIUM-LOW**. It depends on deposit-loss data from commercial surveys and on untested payment and distribution hypotheses.
